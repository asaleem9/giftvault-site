# Storylines

Three films, one picked. Built without a review stop at the owner's request.

## 1. The drawer (picked)

A moment in someone's day, told as an object-to-object chain. The viewer sees a drawer's worth of gift cards dumped across the frame, one of them tagged "expires in 7 days". The cards gather into a phone, and from then on everything on screen is the real app doing the real task: add a Sephora card, spend $5 on Starbucks, flip the card to a barcode at the register. Feels like relief.

- First two seconds: fourteen merchant-coloured cards lie scattered and tilted on bottle green; the Target card carries an orange "expires in 7 days" tag.
- Beats: hook (the drawer) → tension (the tag) → reveal (cards gather into the phone, "Every gift card, one place") → proof (scan or type it once; log what you spend) → payoff (the flip to a barcode floods the frame to white) → claim (no store lets apps read balances; free, no accounts) → end card.
- Look: bottle green #0F2A22 ground with one radial lift, Inter 700 titles at 84 px, one brass accent, real screenshots and clips in a plain CSS phone.
- Sound: silent delivery unless a free bed is available locally; sounds only on the three visible actions (the gather, the flip, the mark).

## 2. At the register

The result first. Open on a cashier's scanner beam across a phone showing the barcode; pull back to find the app and the balance behind it. Form: an endless zoom, each detail turning out to be the next screen. Strongest picture, weakest story: the viewer never sees why they would need it.

## 3. The honest ledger

Type carries the argument. Giant kinetic lines on green ("No store lets apps read balances.") cut on a beat grid, real numbers landing ($42.10 → $37.10), the app appearing only in the proof beat. Bold and cheap to build, but the app itself is on screen too little for a launch.
